## 1. Adobe's Creative Cloud and Business Model

Adobe Creative Cloud is a collection of professional applications used for graphic design, photography, video editing, and digital publishing. Products include Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, Lightroom, and After Effects.

Adobe primarily provides access to these applications through paid subscription plans, offering individual applications and software bundles.

**Key points:**
- Adobe's products are used across professional creative industries.
- Creative Cloud uses recurring subscription plans.
- The company's established products offer extensive features, integrations, and professional workflows.
- Subscription-based access creates recurring revenue for Adobe but also ongoing costs for customers.

**Industry relevance:** Adobe represents an established software company whose competitive advantages include mature applications, professional functionality, and an integrated software ecosystem.

## 2. Adobe's Integration of Generative AI

Adobe has increasingly incorporated generative AI into its creative applications through Adobe Firefly and other AI-powered features.

For example, Photoshop's Generative Fill allows users to add, remove, or modify image content through AI-generated results.

Adobe also offers generative AI functionality through its Creative Cloud plans, including features governed by generative credit allowances.

**Key points:**
- Adobe is incorporating AI into existing creative workflows.
- AI can assist with repetitive or time-consuming creative tasks.
- Adobe is using AI to expand the functionality of its established products.
- AI features may provide additional value to customers and help Adobe remain competitive.

**Industry relevance:** Adobe demonstrates that large software companies are not simply being threatened by AI; they are also using it to strengthen their products.

## 3. What Is ArtCraft?

ArtCraft is an open-source software initiative led by developer Brandon Thomas. In October 2026, the project received attention for introducing alternatives to seven major Adobe applications.

Unlike Adobe's commercial software, these alternatives are available as free, open-source projects.

| ArtCraft application | Adobe application |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| PhotoCraft           | Photoshop         |
| VectorCraft          | Illustrator       |
| FilmCraft            | Premiere Pro      |
| LightCraft           | Lightroom         |
| EffectCraft          | After Effects     |
| DesignCraft          | InDesign          |
| PDFCraft             | Acrobat           | 

The applications were developed using Rust, a programming language commonly used for performance-sensitive software.

**Key distinction:** ArtCraft's developer describes the applications as independent, clean-room reimplementations intended to recreate adobe's similar functionality.

## 4. How AI Coding Tools Contributed to ArtCraft

According to reporting by Ars Technica, Brandon Thomas used Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 to assist in developing the ArtCraft applications.

AI-assisted development helped with creating software intended to reproduce the interfaces and functionality of existing professional applications.

**Key points:**
- Claude was used as a coding assistant during development.
- The applications were written in Rust.
- The project aims to recreate functionality without directly copying Adobe's original source code.
- The code is publicly accessible through GitHub.

**Industry relevance:** ArtCraft illustrates how AI coding tools may help individual developers undertake projects involving complex applications that would traditionally require substantial development resources.

However, the project does not establish that AI can independently produce fully functional professional software without human expertise, testing, and ongoing development.

## 5. Comparing ArtCraft and Adobe

ArtCraft's rapid development and open-source approach have attracted attention, but independent testing has identified considerable limitations.

TechRadar tested ArtCraft applications and reported usability and functionality problems.

**Reported limitations:**
- PhotoCraft experienced problems with certain image formats and saving functionality.
- FilmCraft lacked important editing capabilities and had problems handling audio.
- Several applications remain in early development.
- ArtCraft does not currently offer the same maturity and professional functionality as Adobe's established products.

**Potential advantages of ArtCraft:**
- Free access to the applications
- Open-source code
- Development intended for multiple operating systems
- Opportunities for community contributions and improvements

**Analysis:** ArtCraft demonstrates the potential of AI-assisted development but also highlights the difference between rapidly generating software and creating reliable, production-ready applications.

## 6. Broader Implications for the Software Industry

The Adobe and ArtCraft comparison reflects two ways AI may influence the software industry.

**For established companies:**
- AI can improve existing products and workflows.
- Companies may need to continue innovating to justify subscription prices.
- Mature features, reliability, integrations, and customer support remain competitive advantages.

**For independent developers:**
- AI coding tools may reduce some barriers to developing sophisticated applications.
- Open-source alternatives may become faster to develop.
- Smaller development teams could become more competitive in certain software markets.

**Analysis:** ArtCraft does not demonstrate that AI-created alternatives have already replaced established commercial software.

Instead, it raises the possibility that AI-assisted development could reduce the effort required to create competing applications, potentially changing the competitive landscape over time. 

## 7. Key Research Findings

1. Adobe is integrating generative AI to improve its existing creative software.
2. ArtCraft demonstrates a different use of AI: assisting with the creation of alternatives to established commercial applications.
3. AI may reduce some development barriers for independent developers.
4. ArtCraft's current limitations show that generating software quickly is not equivalent to creating mature, reliable applications.
5. Established companies may face new competition while retaining advantages in functionality, stability, integration, and support.

**Conclusion:** Adobe and ArtCraft highlight an interesting contradiction in the software industry. While established companies like Adobe are heavily investing in AI to improve their products and increase profitability, the same technology is allowing independent developers to create free alternatives to their software. If AI continues to make developing complex applications easier and more accessible, consumers may begin questioning why they should pay expensive subscriptions when comparable alternatives could be available for free. Ironically, the technology these companies are embracing could eventually threaten the very business models they rely on. Although ArtCraft is not yet a direct replacement for Adobe's professional applications, it raises an important question about whether AI could ultimately undermine the competitive advantages of the companies investing in it.
