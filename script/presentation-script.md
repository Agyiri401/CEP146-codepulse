# How AI Coding Tools Are Changing the Future of Software Companies

## Presenter 1 — AI and the Software Industry
*Slides 1–3 | Approximately 1 minute 20 seconds*

### Slide 1 — Introduction

Artificial intelligence is changing how software is developed, but it could also change the companies that develop it.

Traditionally, creating complex software required significant financial resources, experienced developers, and years of development.

Today, AI coding tools are making some of these processes faster and more accessible, raising questions about how established software companies will compete in the future.

### Slide 2 — AI-Assisted Development

Tools such as GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, and Cursor can help developers write, debug, and test code.

According to Google's 2025 DORA report, 90% of surveyed technology professionals reported using AI at work, while over 80% believed it improved their productivity.

This suggests smaller teams and independent developers may be able to accomplish more with fewer resources, potentially lowering barriers to entering the software industry.

### Slide 3 — Impact on Software Companies

As developing software becomes more accessible, established companies could face increased competition from smaller developers offering affordable or open-source alternatives.

This could also challenge subscription-based business models.

Deloitte's 2026 research found that nearly two-thirds of surveyed consumers were frustrated with rising entertainment subscription prices.

Although this research doesn't specifically examine software subscriptions, it highlights how cost can influence consumer decisions.

So what happens when people have access to cheaper alternatives?

## Presenter 2 — Adobe vs. ArtCraft
*Slides 4–6 | Approximately 1 minute 25–35 seconds*

### Slide 4 — Adobe and ArtCraft

Adobe provides an interesting example of this shift.

While Adobe has been investing in generative AI features for applications such as Photoshop and Premiere Pro, independent developer Brandon Thomas used AI coding tools to create ArtCraft.

ArtCraft consists of seven free, open-source alternatives to popular Adobe applications.

This creates an interesting contrast: Adobe is using AI to improve the software it sells, while an independent developer is using AI to create potential competitors.

### Slide 5 — Limitations of AI-Generated Software

Independent testing of ArtCraft has identified missing features, functionality issues, and usability problems. So this isn't to say that ArtCraft is ready to replace Adobe's professional applications just yet.

Google's DORA report also found that approximately 30% of respondents had little or no trust in AI-generated code.

While AI can accelerate certain development tasks, human oversight, testing, and maintenance remain essential.

Established companies still have advantages in reliability, functionality, and professional support.

### Slide 6 — Conclusion

ArtCraft demonstrates how AI-assisted development could make creating complex software more accessible, potentially introducing new competition for established companies.

What's interesting is that the same technology these companies are investing in to improve their products could also make it easier for independent developers to compete with them.

Although AI-generated alternatives still have limitations, the technology is advancing rapidly. ArtCraft was only released recently, this October 2026. While open-source alternatives haven't replaced established software like adobe, projects like ArtCraft raise an important question:

**If comparable software becomes available for free, how will established companies continue to justify the cost of their products?**
